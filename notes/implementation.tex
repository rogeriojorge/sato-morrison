\begin{abstract}
These notes derive and audit a small fixed-field JAX prototype of
magnetic-moment-constrained collisions. Implemented paths include the local
linear weak operator, a positive nodal nonlinear entropy step, five analytic
vacuum fields, tangent toroidal Hamiltonian transport, and an explicit
finite-range surrogate. Independent homogeneous controls include Lorentz and
Dougherty evolution, physical three-velocity Landau weak moments, and direct
screened binary encounters. A checked finite-range Fourier calculation shows
how spatial localization creates a density nullspace and how Gaussian range
lifts it with quartic long-wave decay. Geometric identities, finite-grid
conservation and the selected toroidal observable refinements are verified;
other velocity-tail convergence and physical collision-rate calibration remain
separate requirements. No calibrated dipole confinement,
universal zero-set prescription, full Landau kinetic evolution, or publication
priority is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and equation map}

The primary source is Ref.~\cite{SM25}. Its Eq.~(35) gives a one-species
noncanonical collision structure. Equations (111) and (119) are different levels
of description: the former evolves a ten-coordinate pair distribution, while the
latter closes two five-coordinate species distributions after a pair-factorization
assumption. Equation (181) is a localized linear approximation with a prescribed
scalar collision coefficient. This prototype must identify which of these models
it implements.

The starting model has a prescribed, nonzero vacuum magnetic field, no magnetic
perturbation, a fixed electrostatic reference potential, and one species. The
nonlinear local-kernel extension is named separately from literal Eq.~(181).
No absolute collision rate or laboratory confinement result is established by
these calculations.

\section{Coordinates, measures and the bracket}

We use SI variables before nondimensionalization. Let $u$ be parallel velocity,
$\mu=m v_\perp^2/(2B)$ the magnetic moment, and $\eta=\mu B$ the perpendicular
kinetic energy. With $\bm b=\bm B/B$,
\begin{equation}
 \bm B^*=\bm B+\frac{m u}{q}\nabla\times\bm b,
 \qquad B_\parallel^*=\bm b\cdot\bm B^*.
 \label{eq:bstar}
\end{equation}
For the vacuum backgrounds used in Ref.~\cite{SM25},
$\bm b\cdot\nabla\times\bm b=0$, hence $B_\parallel^*=B$. This does not imply
$\nabla\times\bm b=0$: curvature terms in $\bm B^*$ generally remain.

In coordinates $z=(\X,u,\mu)$ the gyrophase-independent bracket acts on a
covector $a=(\bm a_X,a_u,a_\mu)$ as
\begin{equation}
 \J_\mu a=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 (\bm b\times\bm a_X)/(qB)+\bm B^*a_u/(mB)\\
 -\bm B^*\cdot\bm a_X/(mB)\\0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:jmu}
\end{equation}
The Hamiltonian and characteristic equations are
\begin{align}
 E&=\frac12m u^2+\mu B+q\Phi,\label{eq:energy}\\
 \dot\X&=u\frac{\bm B^*}{B}
 +\frac{\bm b\times(\mu\nabla B+q\nabla\Phi)}{qB},\label{eq:xdot}\\
 \dot u&=-\frac{\bm B^*\cdot(\mu\nabla B+q\nabla\Phi)}{mB},
 \qquad \dot\mu=0.\label{eq:udot}
\end{align}
The signs in \eqref{eq:jmu} must be checked against the cross-field drift in
\eqref{eq:xdot}, not inferred from a memorized matrix convention.

The phase-space measures are
\begin{equation}
 \dd\Gamma=B\,\dd^3X\,\dd u\,\dd\mu
 =\dd^3X\,\dd u\,\dd\eta.
 \label{eq:measure}
\end{equation}
We follow the article's normalization: $n(\X)=\int f\,\dd u\,\dd\eta$. If $F$
is an ordinary gyrotropic velocity density normalized with $\dd^3v$, then
$\dd^3v=(2\pi B/m)\dd u\dd\mu$ and $f=(2\pi/m)F$. Track this constant,
particularly in multispecies comparisons.

The coordinate change $\zeta=(\X,u,\eta)$ has derivative
\begin{equation}
 T=\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial z}
 =\begin{pmatrix}I_3&0&0\\0&1&0\\
 \mu(\nabla B)^T&0&B\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \J_\eta=T\J_\mu T^T.
 \label{eq:transform}
\end{equation}
This constructs the matrix in SM25 (93), equivalently (182), without duplicating
its entries by hand. It also gives
\begin{equation}
 \J_\eta\nabla_\zeta(\eta/B)=0,
 \qquad \dot\eta=\mu\dot\X\cdot\nabla B.
 \label{eq:etadot}
\end{equation}
Thus a discretization that freezes $\eta$ during spatial transport is not the
magnetic-moment-preserving model in a nonuniform field.

With general coordinates and measure $\rho(z)\dd z$, conservative divergence
means $\rho^{-1}\partial_a(\rho F^a)$, not $\partial_aF^a$. Verify the Liouville
identity $\partial_a(\rho\J^{ab})=0$. The prototype uses $\rho=B$ in the $\mu$
chart and $\rho=1$ in the article's $\eta$ chart.

\subsection*{Continuous geometric audit}

The implemented analytic fields pass automatic-differentiation checks of
$\nabla\cdot\bm B=0$, $\nabla\times\bm B=0$, antisymmetry, the magnetic-moment
Casimir, the Liouville density, and the Jacobi identity in both charts, at
independent off-axis points and for both charge signs. Centered differences of
$\bm B$ show second-order convergence to the automatic derivatives for the
rational fields. These sampled identities audit the continuous formulas; they
do not prove a discrete Jacobi identity for the collision mesh or positivity
of $B$ throughout an unsampled domain. Host validation rejects unsupported
fields, nonfinite inputs, zero field, excluded dipole/toroidal sources,
nonpositive mass, zero charge and negative moment.

For the vacuum toroidal field $B=C/R$, the characteristic equations give
$\dot R=\dot u=\dot\mu=0$, $\dot\theta=u/R$ and
$\dot z=(m u^2+\mu B)/(qC)$. Thus annular radial boundaries are tangent to
Hamiltonian flow; a periodic $z$ and toroidal angle are compatible with this
geometry. The library checks this identity and the uniform electrostatic drift
independently of collision evolution.

\subsection*{Normalization is part of the closure}

Choose fixed scales $L_*,v_*,E_*,t_*$ and a diagonal coordinate scale matrix $S$.
For $\zeta=S\hat\zeta$, $E=E_*\hat E$ and $t=t_*\hat t$,
\begin{equation}
 \hat\J=t_*E_*S^{-1}\J S^{-T}.
 \label{eq:dimensionless}
\end{equation}
State these scales with every dimensional result. A Euclidean phase-space
projector applied to a vector containing spatial velocities, accelerations and
energy rates is not defined independently of those scales.

For a purely affine chart change $y=A z$ with time and energy conventions fixed,
a pair vector transforms as $d_y=A d_z$. Its quadratic form is unchanged only if
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_y=A^{-T}\Pi_zA^{-1},\qquad
 d_y^T\Pi_y e_y=d_z^T\Pi_z e_z.
 \label{eq:kerneltransform}
\end{equation}
Recreating an identity matrix and Euclidean projector in the new chart generally
specifies a different kernel. For the position-dependent map \eqref{eq:transform},
compute the two vectors in one common chart before subtracting:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\eta\phi
 =T(z)\J_\mu(z)\nabla_z\phi
 -T(z')\J_\mu(z')\nabla_{z'}\phi'.
 \label{eq:commonchart}
\end{equation}
This is the safe route when storing the state on $\mu$ nodes but reproducing a
kernel defined in the $\eta$ chart.

\section{Nonlinear collision structure and conservation}

For a single species define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\phi=\J\nabla\phi-\J'\nabla'\phi',\qquad \xi=\Delta E.
 \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
A specified kernel must obey exchange symmetry, symmetry of its quadratic form,
positive semidefiniteness and the energy degeneracy:
\begin{equation}
 \Pi=\Pi^T\succeq0,\qquad \Pi\xi=0.
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
The source strong form is
\begin{equation}
 C[f]=\nabla_z\cdot\left[
 f\J\int f'\Pi(\J'\nabla'\ln f'-\J\nabla\ln f)\,\dd z'\right]
 \label{eq:strong}
\end{equation}
in a flat invariant-measure chart. Integration by parts, antisymmetry of $\J$
and exchange of the two labels give
\begin{equation}
 \int\phi C[f]\dd\Gamma
 =-\frac12\iint ff'\,\Delta\phi^T\Pi\Delta\ln f\,
 \dd\Gamma\dd\Gamma'.
 \label{eq:weak}
\end{equation}
All following equalities require the boundary terms to vanish. The paper states
conditions for both the total flux and the Hamiltonian part; a finite numerical
box does not satisfy them automatically.

Set $\phi=1$ in \eqref{eq:weak} to obtain particle conservation. Set $\phi=E$
and use \eqref{eq:kernel} for collision energy conservation. Since
$\J\nabla g(\mu)=0$ for every smooth $g$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int f g(\mu)\dd\Gamma=0.
 \label{eq:casimirs}
\end{equation}
For closed evolution this is equivalent to conserving the full marginal
\begin{equation}
 G(\mu)=\int B f(\X,u,\mu)\dd^3X\dd u.
 \label{eq:marginal}
\end{equation}
It is stronger than conservation of $\int\mu f\dd\Gamma$ alone.

Define $S[f]=-\int f\ln(f/f_{\rm ref})\dd\Gamma$ with a constant reference
scale inside the logarithm. Particle conservation removes that scale from the
entropy rate. Equation \eqref{eq:weak} yields
\begin{equation}
 \dot S=\frac12\iint ff'\Delta\ln f^T\Pi\Delta\ln f\,
 \dd\Gamma\dd\Gamma'\ge0.
 \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}
These are structural statements from the model, not a proof of a unique final
state. Additional null directions of $\Pi$ can prevent mixing.

A candidate observable $p$ is conserved by the full kinetic model only if the
Hamiltonian flow preserves it and the collision form annihilates $\Delta p$.
An external dipole field does not imply conservation of all Cartesian momenta.
For an axisymmetric model, check canonical angular momentum using the actual
kernel and boundaries rather than inferring it from the field name.

\section{The fixed-field linear model}

Write $f=f_0(1+h)$, where $f_0>0$ is stationary. For the source stationary family,
\begin{equation}
 f_0=A\exp[-\beta E_0+g(\mu)],
 \qquad \J\nabla\ln f_0=-\beta\J\nabla E_0.
 \label{eq:fzero}
\end{equation}
With the fields and kernel fixed, the zeroth-order collision term vanishes.
This supplied energy/moment family is not claimed to exhaust stationary
states. For the exactly local poloidal surrogate,
\eqref{eq:fluxstationaryfamily} adds an arbitrary conserved-flux function.
The first-order weak form is
\begin{equation}
 \int\phi C_L[f_0h]\dd\Gamma
 =-\frac12\iint f_0f_0'\Delta\phi^T\Pi_0\Delta h\,
 \dd\Gamma\dd\Gamma'.
 \label{eq:linearweak}
\end{equation}
The local scalar approximation in SM25 (181) uses
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_0=D P I_x P\,\delta^3(\X-\X'),\quad
 P=I-\frac{\xi_0\xi_0^T}{\xi_0^T\xi_0},\quad
 I_x=\diag(1,1,1,0,0).
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
Here $D$ is a prescribed constant, and the projector is evaluated in a specified
normalized common chart. The two velocity integrals remain after the spatial
delta function is used. In a $\mu$ chart, the local pair measure contains two
velocity Jacobians; do not accidentally retain only one factor of $B$.

The perturbation norm
\begin{equation}
 Q[h]=\frac12\int f_0 h^2\dd\Gamma
 \label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \dot Q=-\frac12\iint f_0f_0'\Delta h^T\Pi_0\Delta h\,
 \dd\Gamma\dd\Gamma'\le0
 \label{eq:qrate}
\end{equation}
for collision-only dynamics. A compatible stationary Hamiltonian flow also
preserves $Q$ under suitable boundary conditions.

For self-consistent fields, \eqref{eq:linearweak} is not a complete linearization
unless the omitted terms have been ordered out. Differentiating the exact
energy-degeneracy condition gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta\Pi\,\xi_0+\Pi_0\delta\xi=0.
 \label{eq:deltapi}
\end{equation}
The approximations in SM25 (164)--(170) must be carried explicitly into any
extension. The fixed-field implementation checks its linear operator against
a directional derivative of the nonlinear residual; this does not validate
omitted self-consistent-field terms.

\section{Uniform-field reduction and exact null modes}

This section is an independent derivation from the source Eq.~(181). Take a
uniform field, a constant reference potential and $f_0=f_0(u,\eta)$. For
$\bm b=\bm e_z$,
\begin{equation}
 \J a=\left(-\frac{a_y}{qB},\frac{a_x}{qB},
 \frac{a_u}{m},-\frac{a_z}{m},0\right)^T,
 \qquad \J\nabla E_0=(0,0,u,0,0)^T.
 \label{eq:uniformj}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\xi_0=(u-u')\bm e_z$ in the spatial block, and
\begin{equation}
 P I_xP=\diag(1,1,0,0,0).
 \label{eq:uniformp}
\end{equation}
At $u=u'$ use the continuous limit \eqref{eq:uniformp}; this prescription does not
extend automatically to a general nonuniform field.

Only perpendicular spatial derivatives survive. Let
\begin{equation}
 n_0=\int f_0\dd u\dd\eta,\qquad
 \delta n=\int\delta f\dd u\dd\eta.
\end{equation}
Two applications of the cross-product matrix give
$(\bm b\times)^2=-I_\perp$. Substitution into the strong form gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{C_L[\delta f]=\frac{D}{(qB)^2}\nabla_\perp^2
 (n_0\delta f-f_0\delta n).}
 \label{eq:uniformreduction}
\end{equation}
This is spatial mixing of velocity-dependent structure, not ordinary homogeneous
velocity-space thermalization.

For a Fourier mode with $\bm k_\perp=\bm k-(\bm k\cdot\bm b)\bm b$, define
\begin{equation}
 \lambda=\frac{Dn_0|\bm k_\perp|^2}{(qB)^2},\qquad
 \bar h=\frac{1}{n_0}\int f_0h\dd u\dd\eta.
\end{equation}
The collision-only equation is
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t h=-\lambda(h-\bar h),\qquad
 h(t)=\bar h+[h(0)-\bar h]e^{-\lambda t}.
 \label{eq:exactdecay}
\end{equation}
Thus a density-like perturbation is undamped. If $\bm k_\perp=0$, every velocity
perturbation is collisionally undamped. A homogeneous anisotropic distribution
is therefore not an isotropization benchmark for this model.

With positive velocity quadrature weights $w_i$, set
\begin{equation}
 m_i=w_i f_{0i},\quad s_i=\sqrt{m_i},\quad n_0=\sum_i m_i.
\end{equation}
The matrix for $h$ and its entropy-scaled form are
\begin{align}
 L_h&=-\lambda\left(I-\frac{\bm 1\bm m^T}{n_0}\right),\\
 M_v^{1/2}L_hM_v^{-1/2}
 &=-\lambda\left(I-\frac{\bm s\bm s^T}{n_0}\right).
 \label{eq:uniformspectrum}
\end{align}
For $\lambda>0$ the spectrum has one zero eigenvalue and $N_v-1$ copies of
$-\lambda$. This is an exact finite-quadrature statement when the same $n_0$ is
used throughout. It provides a stringent factor, sign and normalization test.

When parallel streaming is added, $\partial_t h=-ik_\parallel uh+L_hh$.
A density-like collision null mode need not remain in that nullspace under
streaming. Distinguish the collision spectrum from the spectrum of the combined
generator. With $\bm k_\perp=0$ and frozen fields the exact streaming solution is
$h(t,u,\eta)=h(0,u,\eta)e^{-ik_\parallel ut}$; this is not self-consistent Landau
damping.

\section{A finite-range surrogate and its exact density branch}

This section specifies a new calculation for an explicit surrogate, not a
Coulomb scattering tensor or a claim of publication priority. Retain the uniform
field and constant-potential assumptions of \eqref{eq:uniformj}, and replace the
spatial delta function in \eqref{eq:local} by a nonnegative, even, translation
invariant kernel $W(\X-\X')$ with $\int W\dd^3X=1$. Use the same uniform
$P I_xP=I_\perp$ prescription, including its continuous equal-$u$ limit.
The weak form then gives
\begin{equation}
 C_W[\delta f]=\frac{D}{(qB)^2}\nabla_\perp^2
 \left[n_0\delta f-f_0(W*\delta n)\right].
 \label{eq:rangereduction}
\end{equation}
To check the sign and convolution placement, the projected pair difference for
$h$ is $(\bm b\times)/(qB)$ applied to
$\nabla_\perp h(\X,v)-\nabla'_{\perp}h(\X',v')$. The first self term integrates
to $n_0\nabla_\perp h$; the cross term is $\nabla_\perp W*\delta n$.
The second cross-product operation contributes $-I_\perp$, giving
\eqref{eq:rangereduction} with the displayed diffusion sign.

\textbf{Finite-quadrature proposition.} Let $m_i=w_i f_{0i}>0$, $n_0=\sum_i m_i$,
$s_i=\sqrt{m_i}$, $r(\bm k)=\widehat W(\bm k)$, and
$\lambda=Dn_0|\bm k_\perp|^2/(qB)^2$. In the entropy coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 -M_v^{1/2}L_{W,\bm k}M_v^{-1/2}
 =\lambda\left[I-r(\bm k)\frac{\bm s\bm s^T}{n_0}\right].
 \label{eq:rangespectrum}
\end{equation}
Its exact decay rates are $\lambda$ on the $N_v-1$ dimensional velocity-neutral
subspace and $\lambda[1-r(\bm k)]$ on the density direction $\bm s$.
Indeed, $\bm s^T\bm s=n_0$, so the normalized outer product is an orthogonal
rank-one projector. An even nonnegative kernel has real $r\le1$; thus all
rates are nonnegative. This proof does not require $r\ge0$.
For a discrete periodic spatial quadrature the identical statement holds with
$r$ equal to the Fourier multiplier of the sampled, symmetrically normalized
kernel, rather than the continuum multiplier.

For a normalized periodized isotropic Gaussian of covariance $\ell^2 I$,
$r=e^{-\ell^2|\bm k|^2/2}$ and hence
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\rm density}
 =\frac{Dn_0|\bm k_\perp|^2}{(qB)^2}
   \left(1-e^{-\ell^2|\bm k|^2/2}\right)
 =\frac{Dn_0\ell^2}{2(qB)^2}|\bm k_\perp|^2|\bm k|^2
  +O(|\bm k_\perp|^2\ell^4|\bm k|^4).
 \label{eq:quartic}
\end{equation}
A transverse long wave therefore has quartic rather than quadratic density
decay. Every finite nonzero Gaussian range lifts the nonzero-transverse-wave
local density null mode, but the lifted rate vanishes as $\ell^2$ in the local
limit. Homogeneous and purely parallel modes remain fully collisionally null.
Consequently this calculation does not establish a whole-space spectral gap,
or a gap for all modes on a periodic domain containing parallel-only modes.
On a fixed finite set of transverse Fourier modes, it establishes the stated
positive density rates. The limits $t\to\infty$ and $\ell\to0$ need not commute
for the density branch.

The independent NumPy check in \texttt{examples/06\_range.py} assembles the
ordered spatial/velocity pair weak sum, including the factor $1/2$. The
Fourier basis is differentiated exactly; the Gaussian is evaluated by positive
periodic image sums and normalized with one scalar to retain pair symmetry.
Three spatial resolutions, three velocity node counts and three kernel ranges
are compared with \eqref{eq:rangespectrum}. The velocity weights are normalized
once at setup to fixed $n_0$; this verifies the finite-quadrature theorem rather
than physical velocity-tail convergence. The result is independent of the
production JAX pair implementation.

\subsection*{Literature comparison and novelty limits}

SM25 supplies the noncanonical weak structure and its local scalar approximation.
SM24 supplies the earlier noncanonical construction~\cite{SM24}. BS25, Eqs.~(99)--(103),
retains magnetic-moment friction and diffusion through its gyrophase-dependent
projection $\Delta^\mu_{\rm gc}$, and explains the structural reduction obtained
by removing that component and replacing the relative velocity by the reduced
Hamiltonian pair velocity. That passage does not identify every scalar/local
SM25 approximation with the complete conventional Landau tensor. Our uniform
surrogate remains unable to isotropize a homogeneous distribution.

KH17 supplies the discrete-gradient conservation and entropy method; those
properties do not supply a coercivity constant. Bressan, Kraus, Maj and Morrison
\cite{BKMM25} analyze sufficient relaxation conditions, including additional
finite-dimensional inequalities and tentative infinite-dimensional extensions.
Their framework motivates testing the actual nullspace before assigning
asymptotic decay to a constrained maximum-entropy state.

On 7 October 2026, primary-source searches of the SM25 title combined with
finite range, nullspace and Gaussian terms, and searches for noncanonical
finite-range collisions and magnetic-moment quartic relaxation, found no
matching statement of \eqref{eq:rangespectrum}--\eqref{eq:quartic}. Local full-text
checks covered SM25, SM24, BS25, KH17 and BBQ15. This is a scoped independently
checked derivation for the specified surrogate; absence from these searches is
not proof of publication priority. No general nonuniform finite-range theorem,
Coulomb rate, or physical metastability is inferred.

\section{Stationary states and initial constraints}

The source stationary family \eqref{eq:fzero} follows from energy degeneracy and
the magnetic-moment Casimir. For $g(\mu)=-\beta\gamma\mu$,
\begin{equation}
 f_*=A\exp\left[-\beta\left(\frac12m u^2+
 \mu(B+\gamma)+q\Phi\right)\right],\quad B+\gamma>0.
 \label{eq:linearg}
\end{equation}
Integrating over $u$ and $\mu$ using \eqref{eq:measure} gives
\begin{equation}
 n_*(\X)=A\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\beta m}}\,
 e^{-\beta q\Phi}\frac{B}{\beta(B+\gamma)}.
 \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
The corresponding temperature parameters in energy units are
\begin{equation}
 T_\parallel=\beta^{-1},\qquad
 T_\perp=\frac{B}{\beta(B+\gamma)}.
 \label{eq:temperatures}
\end{equation}
These formulas are useful checks of velocity integration and the definition of
pressure. They do not establish that an arbitrary initial state evolves to this
particular family member.

For a prescribed fixed-field energy and a known initial marginal $G_0(\mu)$,
let
\begin{equation}
 Z_\mu(\beta)=\int B(\X)e^{-\beta E(\X,u,\mu)}\dd^3X\dd u.
\end{equation}
A candidate constrained maximum-entropy state is
\begin{equation}
 f_*(\X,u,\mu)=\frac{G_0(\mu)}{Z_\mu(\beta)}e^{-\beta E(\X,u,\mu)}.
 \label{eq:generalsteady}
\end{equation}
Solve for $\beta$ from total energy. Add other invariant multipliers if they
actually survive the chosen dynamics. The same initial inverse-temperature
parameter need not equal the final multiplier. Extra kernel invariants can make
\eqref{eq:generalsteady} inaccessible.

At fixed $G_0$, differentiation of the mean energy of
\eqref{eq:generalsteady} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd E_*}{\dd\beta}
 =-\int G_0(\mu)\operatorname{Var}_{\beta,\mu}(E)\dd\mu\le0.
 \label{eq:betamonotone}
\end{equation}
It is strictly negative if at least one populated moment bin has nonconstant
energy. Thus an energy target within the attainable bracket selects a unique
$\beta$ in that bracket. Positive-temperature existence is not automatic for
an arbitrary truncated initial distribution; the implementation rejects an
unbracketed target. The same calculation holds for positive finite quadrature.
With matching energy and full moment marginal, relative entropy gives
$S[f_*]-S[f]=\int f\ln(f/f_*)\dd\Gamma\ge0$. This identifies a constrained
maximum-entropy candidate; it does not establish dynamic accessibility under
an operator possessing additional invariants.

For self-consistent electrostatics,
\begin{equation}
 -\epsilon_0\nabla^2\Phi=\sum_s q_sn_s+\rho_{\rm fixed}.
 \label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
The self-field energy is $\epsilon_0\int|\nabla\Phi|^2\dd^3X/2$, with boundary
terms treated consistently. A prescribed external potential contributes
$\sum_s\int q_s\Phi_{\rm ext}f_s\dd\Gamma_s$ without the self-interaction
half-factor. A periodic field solve requires net neutrality and a gauge choice.

\section{Comparison models and what each tests}

The conventional reduced Landau literature retains magnetic-moment friction and
diffusion even when the distribution is gyrophase independent
\cite{B04,BBQ15,HBP17,BS25}. Brizard and Sugama explicitly discuss a structural
reduction with $\bm\Delta_{\rm gc}^{\mu}=0$ and a reduced relative velocity in
Sec.~V of Ref.~\cite{BS25}. This is a bridge for comparison, not a verification
that every simplified Sato--Morrison kernel is identical to their Landau tensor.
The papers use different conventions; convert all masses, charges and unit
factors before comparing implementations.

\subsection*{Pitch-angle scattering}

With $v=|\bm v|$ and $\xi=u/v$, use
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm Lz}[F]=\frac{\nu(v)}{2}\partial_\xi
 [(1-\xi^2)\partial_\xi F].
 \label{eq:lorentz}
\end{equation}
The Legendre identity gives $C_{\rm Lz}[P_\ell]=
-\nu\ell(\ell+1)P_\ell/2$ for constant $\nu$ on a speed shell. Hence degrees
$0,1,2,3$ have rates $0,\nu,3\nu,6\nu$. Speed-shell population and energy are
conserved, but momentum of the evolved species can be transferred to the fixed
reservoir. Since $\mu=m v^2(1-\xi^2)/(2B)$, this is a moment-changing comparison.
Derive its coordinate transform; it is not bare diffusion at fixed $u$ in $\mu$.

\subsection*{A nonlinear conserving Fokker--Planck model}

The single-species Dougherty form used as a comparison is
\begin{equation}
 C_D[F]=\nu\nabla_v\cdot[(\bm v-\bm U)F+\theta\nabla_vF],
 \quad \bm U=\frac1n\int\bm vF\dd^3v,
 \quad \theta=\frac{1}{3n}\int|\bm v-\bm U|^2F\dd^3v.
 \label{eq:dougherty}
\end{equation}
Integration by parts shows number and momentum conservation. The trace of the
centered covariance is constant. Its matrix satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \dot\Theta=-2\nu(\Theta-\theta I),\qquad
 \Theta(t)=\theta I+[\Theta(0)-\theta I]e^{-2\nu t}.
 \label{eq:covariance}
\end{equation}
For an initially Gaussian homogeneous state this specifies the full Gaussian
solution. Use it as an exact code test. A fixed bath temperature or drift would
define a different model. Neither version is the exact Coulomb operator.

\subsection*{Physical three-velocity Landau reference}

A one-species normalized reference is
\begin{align}
 U(\bm w)&=\frac{I-\hat{\bm w}\hat{\bm w}^{T}}{|\bm w|},\\
 C_{\rm La}[F]&=\Gamma\nabla_v\cdot\int FF'U(\bm v-\bm v')
 (\nabla_v\ln F-\nabla_{v'}\ln F')\dd^3v'.
 \label{eq:landau}
\end{align}
Derive the dimensional prefactor for the selected normalization separately. Its
positive kernel and velocity-difference null direction support conventional
number, momentum, energy and entropy tests. Use an independently assembled
small 3V problem before implementing a general transformed operator.

A gyrotropic two-coordinate distribution still has three-dimensional collision
geometry. Converge the gyrophase integral or derive its analytic average; a
literal 2V Landau kernel is not an equivalent comparator. Maxwell-molecule and
BKW-type tests in Ref.~\cite{CHWW20} can test a different kernel's numerical
machinery, but do not establish Coulomb rates.

\section{Analytic magnetic fields and boundaries}

A vacuum field can be specified locally as the gradient of a harmonic scalar
potential. Use the following fields with explicit domains.

\textbf{Uniform field.} $\bm B=B_c\bm b_0$, with $|\bm b_0|=1$.

\textbf{Harmonic mirror.} Let $R^2=x^2+y^2$ and
\begin{align}
 \Psi_B&=B_cz+a\left(z^3/3-zR^2/2\right),\\
 \bm B&=\nabla\Psi_B=(-azx,-azy,B_c+a[z^2-R^2/2]).
 \label{eq:mirrorfield}
\end{align}
Its curl and divergence vanish identically. Choose a domain on which $B$ stays
strictly positive. The dimensionless variation is controlled by $aL^2/B_c$.
An arbitrary field $B(z)\bm e_z$ is not an admissible substitute.

\textbf{Toroidal vacuum field.} On $R>R_{\min}>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \bm B=\frac{C}{R^2}(-y,x,0).
 \label{eq:toroid}
\end{equation}
This provides curvature and field-strength variation without a parallel gradient
of $B$. Its noncontractible domain is part of the problem.

\textbf{Point dipole.} Away from the source,
\begin{equation}
 \bm B=\frac{C_d}{r^5}(3xz,3yz,2z^2-x^2-y^2),
 \quad \Psi_B=-C_dz/r^3.
 \label{eq:dipole}
\end{equation}
This is a field test, not a model of a finite winding or material boundary. Exclude
$r=0$ and any near-source region outside the guiding-center ordering.

\textbf{Symmetry breaking.} A controlled addition
$\delta\Psi_B=\varepsilon B_cxy/L$ is harmonic. Its gradient can be added to a
vacuum background; check the resulting nonzero-field domain and boundary setup.

Do not make a nonperiodic magnetic field periodic by wrapping its array. Begin
with collision-only nonuniform tests under natural zero-collision-flux boundary
conditions. For full dynamics, require tangent Hamiltonian flux, a derived
invariant-compatible reflection, a specified confining potential, or an open
system with measured flux budgets. Arbitrary specular reflection need not
preserve $\mu$. A density-profile test without these choices is not a closed
relaxation experiment.

\subsection*{Complete smooth nullspace of the local toroidal surrogate}

The following classification concerns the specified local $P I_XP$ closure,
not a conventional Coulomb operator. Assume $B=(C/R)\bm e_\theta$, $C>0$,
$m>0$, $q\ne0$, constant electrostatic potential and $D>0$.
Take a connected radial interval away from $R=0$, periodic $\theta,z$, and a
product velocity domain $I_u\times I_\mu$ with both intervals of positive
length. The equilibrium weight is strictly positive in this domain.
Consider real $C^2$ test functions in the weighted form domain, so the pair
quadratic integral is finite. Periodicity and natural zero collision flux on
nonperiodic faces give the weak identity \eqref{eq:linearweak}.

\textbf{Proposition.} The smooth collision-null functions are exactly
\begin{equation}
 h=\phi(R,\theta,z)+g(\theta,\mu)
   +a(\theta)E+b(\theta)mRu,
 \qquad E=\tfrac12mu^2+\mu C/R.
 \label{eq:toroidalnullspace}
\end{equation}
Here the functions are arbitrary subject to the stated regularity,
periodicity and integrability. Redundant angle-only terms may be moved between
$\phi$ and $g$. The common collision and ideal stationary functions are exactly
\begin{equation}
 h=\phi(R)+g(\mu)+aE+b mRu,
 \qquad a,b\ \hbox{constant}.
 \label{eq:toroidalcommonnull}
\end{equation}
For a stationary positive $f_0$, skew Hamiltonian transport and the collision
norm identity imply that these also exhaust the smooth zero modes of the
combined linear generator. This classifies stationary null modes; it does not
prove a decay rate, coercivity constant, or spectral gap.

\textbf{Proof of the collision statement.} In the common Cartesian $\eta$ chart,
write the spatial action in the local orthonormal basis
$(\bm e_R,\bm e_\theta,\bm e_z)$:
\begin{equation}
 A_Xh=\left(\frac{R h_z}{qC},\frac{h_u}{m},
                 \frac{-R h_R+u h_u}{qC}\right),\qquad
 V_X=A_XE=\left(0,u,\frac{m u^2+\mu C/R}{qC}\right).
 \label{eq:toroidalaction}
\end{equation}
The factor $m$ in $\bm B^*$ cancels the bracket denominator $m$ in the
curvature term, so the last numerator contains $u h_u$, not $m u h_u$.
The energy characteristic has $\dot u=\dot\eta=0$; thus its pair vector
$\xi$ is spatial-only and $P I_XP$ discards all $u,\eta$ components.
Zero dissipation is consequently equivalent to
$A_Xh(v)-A_Xh(v')$ being parallel to $V_X(v)-V_X(v')$ for all local velocity
pairs. Positivity of the pair weight first gives the statement almost
everywhere; smoothness extends it throughout the interior.

The radial component gives $h_z$ independent of $(u,\mu)$.
The map $(u,\mu)\mapsto(u,(m u^2+\mu C/R)/(qC))$ is invertible onto an open
two-dimensional set. On such a set, a map whose every chord is parallel to the
corresponding argument chord has the form $F(v)=\alpha v+c$ with scalar
$\alpha$ constant on that velocity set. To prove this elementary lemma, fix one
point and compare its chords with two noncollinear chords: parallelism of the
third chord forces their scalar factors to agree. An open set supplies a
noncollinear comparison for each additional point.
Applied to the angular and vertical components of \eqref{eq:toroidalaction},
this gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{h_u}{m}=\alpha(\X)u+c_\theta(\X),\quad
 \frac{-R h_R+u h_u}{qC}
 =\alpha(\X)\frac{m u^2+\mu C/R}{qC}+c_z(\X).
\end{equation}
Integrate the first identity in $u$ to obtain
$h=m\alpha u^2/2+m c_\theta u+d(\X,\mu)$.
Comparing the $u^2$, $u$ and constant terms in the second yields
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_R=0,\qquad (c_\theta)_R=c_\theta/R,\qquad
 d_R=-\alpha\mu C/R^2-qC c_z/R.
\end{equation}
Hence $c_\theta=R b(\theta,z)$ and
$h=\alpha(\theta,z)E+b(\theta,z)mRu+
 g(\theta,z,\mu)+\phi(R,\theta,z)$.
The already obtained velocity independence of $h_z$ forces
$\alpha_z=b_z=0$ and makes $g_z$ independent of $\mu$; its remaining
velocity-independent part can be absorbed into $\phi$.
This proves necessity of \eqref{eq:toroidalnullspace}.
The product velocity domain ensures the integration constant is a globally
single-valued function of $\mu$; connected support alone would not exclude
branchwise functions on disconnected $u$-fibers.

For sufficiency, a spatial-only $\phi$ has velocity-independent spatial action,
a $g(\theta,\mu)$ has zero spatial action, an $a(\theta)E$ has pair spatial
action $a\xi$, and a $b(\theta)mRu$ has spatial action
$b(\theta)R\bm e_\theta$ independent of velocity. In the last identity,
$\bm b\times(m u\bm e_R)/(qB)$ cancels the curvature part from
$\partial_u(mRu)=mR$. Their projected pair differences therefore all vanish.
This also proves that all spatial populations are conserved by local
collisions, including the nonlinear surrogate. Since the local energy-flow
map is injective, distinct toroidal velocity states never have $\xi=0$;
the coincident diagonal has vanishing test-function difference.

\textbf{Proof of the common stationary statement.} The ideal derivative is
$\mathcal T h=(u/R)h_\theta+
[(m u^2+\mu C/R)/(qC)]h_z$.
Substitute \eqref{eq:toroidalnullspace} and regard it as a polynomial in $u$.
The cubic coefficient forces $a'=0$; the constant coefficient
$\mu\phi_z/(qR)$ forces $\phi_z=0$, since the moment interval has nonzero
length. The quadratic coefficient then forces $b'=0$.
The remaining linear coefficient is $\phi_\theta+g_\theta=0$;
angle-only redundant terms can be absorbed between $\phi$ and $g$, giving
\eqref{eq:toroidalcommonnull}. Periodic $z$ supplies the closed boundary for
the norm argument, but is not needed for this pointwise algebraic intersection.
A proposed nonperiodic compensator $\phi=-qC b'(\theta)z$ cancels a quadratic
term but leaves $-\mu C b'/R$, and is not stationary on open moment support.

Thus combined dynamics retain the full radial population, the full moment
marginal, energy and toroidal canonical momentum $mRu$. A general joint
$(R,\mu)$ marginal is not implied. Arbitrary radial profiles cannot approach a
unique state determined only by energy and $G(\mu)$.
The same argument classifies positive nonlinear collision equilibria by taking
$h=\ln f$ when the entropy form is finite; their common ideal stationary family
is $\ln f=\phi(R)+g(\mu)+aE+b mRu$.
Open boundaries with imposed flux, velocity support of dimension one,
vanishing pair weights, disconnected velocity fibers, a nonlocal spatial
kernel, or an electrostatic field that changes the energy-flow components
are outside this proposition.

On the $3^5=243$-node test, 27 spatial indicators, nine angular--moment
indicators, three angular--energy functions and three angular--momentum
functions have rank 39 after three duplicated angle-only functions are counted
once. Their production actions vanish to roundoff and account for all 39
observed near-null eigenvalues of the unmodified matrix. The continuum proof
above is independent of that finite-grid rank check; eigenvalues are not altered.
A separate continuous-bracket check at $m=1.7$, $q=-0.8$ and three off-axis
points verifies random classified functions and the cylindrical action formula.

Primary-source searches of the authors with toroidal collision nullspace,
radial populations and moment constraints, and local review of SM25 and BS25,
did not locate this specific local-kernel classification. Local density and
symmetry-generated invariants are established ideas; publication priority for
this closure-specific classification remains unresolved. No claim about
physical Coulomb transport, general magnetic geometry, or a measured lifetime
follows from it.

\section{Vacuum energy-flow zero sets and spatial population constraints}

The results in this section concern the prescribed local scalar
$D P I_XP$ closure, a time-independent nonzero vacuum field, constant
electrostatic potential, $m>0$, $q\ne0$, and the common Cartesian $\eta$ chart.
The positive scalar $D$ is a symmetric local pair weight and may depend on the local
velocity pair. Statements about
the nullspace require it and the equilibrium density to be strictly positive
almost everywhere. Global conservation statements additionally require the
closed weak collision boundaries used in the entropy identity. These are
continuous statements; a modified discrete energy gradient is a separate
approximation.

\subsection*{Injectivity away from critical magnetic strength}
Set $\mathbf g=\nabla B$, $\mathbf t=\mathbf b\times\mathbf g$ and
$s=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf g$. The vacuum identity
$\nabla\times\mathbf b=\mathbf t/B$ follows by differentiating
$\mathbf b=\mathbf B/B$ and using $\nabla\times\mathbf B=0$.
For $E=mu^2/2+\mu B$, the energy flow in the common chart is exactly
\begin{equation}
 V=T\J_\mu\nabla E=
 \left(u\mathbf b+\frac{mu^2+\mu B}{qB^2}\mathbf t,
       -\frac{\mu s}{m},\ \mu u s\right).
 \label{eq:vacuumflowmap}
\end{equation}
Here $mu^2$ denotes mass times $u^2$, whereas $\mu$ is the magnetic moment.
At fixed spatial position, $\mathbf b\cdot V_X=u$. Equality of two flow
vectors therefore fixes $u$. If $\mathbf t\ne0$, the remaining spatial
component fixes $\mu$. If $\mathbf t=0$ but $s\ne0$, the parallel-velocity
component $V_u=-\mu s/m$ fixes $\mu$. Thus the map $(u,\mu)\mapsto V$ is
injective whenever $\nabla B\ne0$. Its derivative has rank two: the $u$ column
has $\mathbf b\cdot\partial_u V_X=1$, while the $\mu$ column has zero such
projection and is nonzero. Conversely, at $\nabla B=0$ the map is
$V=(u\mathbf b,0,0)$, so distinct equal-$u$ moment states have equal flows.
This classifies the fixed-position zero set without regularizing $|\xi|^2$.
The fixed-position qualification is essential. In the unit dipole with
$m=q=1$, the distinct equatorial states
$(\X,u,\mu)=((1,0,0),0.2,0.5)$ and
$((2,0,0),0.2,0.76)$ both have common-chart energy flow
$(0,1.62,-0.2,0,0)$, even though $\nabla B$ is nonzero at both positions.
At the equator $B=C/R^3$, $\mathbf b=-\mathbf e_z$, and the azimuthal
flow is $3[mu^2R^2/C+\mu/R]/q$. This distinct remote pair also has a nonremovable directional weak limit
for the spatial scalar projector. For $h=u$,
$(Au)_X=-\mathbf e_z+3uR^2\mathbf e_\theta$,
so the pair action difference is $-1.8\mathbf e_\theta$.
Perturbing only $\mu$ at the first state makes $\xi$ parallel to
$\mathbf e_\theta$ and the weak factor vanish. Perturbing only $u$ by
$\delta$ instead gives
\begin{equation}
 \left|I_XP\Delta Au\right|^2
 =\frac{(1.8-3\delta)^2}{1+(1.2+3\delta)^2}
 \longrightarrow\frac{3.24}{2.44}=1.327868852\ldots .
 \label{eq:nonlocaldirectionallimit}
\end{equation}
The actual common-chart action and projection are tested along three
nonsingular values of each perturbation; the singular pair is not assigned
a projector. Thus local injectivity supplies no general equal-flow exclusion
or continuous zero-vector prescription for a nonlocal scalar extension.
This observation alone does not analyze the full interaction tensor in the
nonlocal source Eq.~(119).

The point dipole has no critical strength point away from its source:
homogeneity gives $\X\cdot\nabla B=-3B$. For the mirror, writing
$B_z=C+a(z^2-R^2/2)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_R(B^2)=2aR(-C+aR^2/2),\qquad
 \partial_z(B^2)=4az(C+az^2).
\end{equation}
For $C>0$ and $a\ne0$, simultaneous zeros in the nonzero-field region occur
only at the origin. The executed positive-$z$ box excludes it.
For a dipole plus the degree-one harmonic perturbation, write
$\mathbf B=\mathbf B_d+\mathbf B_p$. Euler homogeneity yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac12\X\cdot\nabla(B^2)
 =\mathbf B\cdot(-3\mathbf B_d+\mathbf B_p)
 \le-3|\mathbf B_d|^2+2|\mathbf B_d||\mathbf B_p|+|\mathbf B_p|^2<0
\end{equation}
whenever $|\mathbf B_p|<|\mathbf B_d|$. The bounds above establish this on the
executed perturbed-dipole box. Hence all three nonuniform Cartesian test
domains have injective continuous local energy-flow maps. This does not by
itself certify injectivity of a finite-grid energy-flow approximation.

For a smooth test function, $\Delta Ah=O(|\Delta(u,\mu)|)$ near a coincident
pair. Since $P I_XP$ is bounded, its weak quadratic factor is
$O(|\Delta(u,\mu)|^2)$ and extends uniquely by zero on the diagonal, even
though the projector itself has a directional limit. In contrast, a
distinct equal-flow pair at a critical spatial point need not have a
continuous weak extension.

\subsection*{A nonremovable critical-point weak limit}
Consider the mirror near its nonzero-field origin, equal $u\ne0$,
$\mu\ne\mu'$, and $h=\mu x$. Approach along the $x$ axis with $z=0$.
Then $\mathbf b=\mathbf e_z$, $\mathbf t$ is parallel to $\mathbf e_y$,
and both $\xi$ and $\Delta Ah$ are spatial and parallel. Therefore
$|I_XP\Delta Ah|^2=0$. Approach instead along the $z$ axis. Now
$\mathbf t=0$ and $\xi$ has only $u$ and $\eta$ components, while
$\Delta Ah=(\mu-\mu')\mathbf e_y/(qB)$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{x\to0,\ z=0}|I_XP\Delta Ah|^2=0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{z\to0,\ x=0}|I_XP\Delta Ah|^2
 =\frac{(\mu-\mu')^2}{q^2C^2}>0.
 \label{eq:criticalweaklimit}
\end{equation}
Varying $u-u'$ at the exact origin gives the latter uniform-field
prescription. Thus a simultaneous small-gradient and equal-$u$ limit is
not equivalent to first imposing the uniform model. There is no universal
continuous extension at these distinct-state critical pairs. An arbitrary
bounded assignment on a measure-zero critical spatial set does not change
an absolutely continuous weak integral, but it cannot be described as its
unique continuous limit. Positive quadrature nodes on such a set need a
separately declared prescription. No denominator softening is justified by
this argument.

\subsection*{Spatial invariants of local collisions}
For a spatial observable $\phi(\X)$ independent of $u,\mu$, its common-chart
action is
\begin{equation}
 A\phi=\left(\frac{\mathbf b\times\nabla\phi}{qB},
 -\frac{\mathbf b\cdot\nabla\phi}{m}
 -\frac{u\,\mathbf t\cdot\nabla\phi}{qB^2},
 -\frac{\mu\,\mathbf t\cdot\nabla\phi}{qB}\right).
 \label{eq:vacuumspatialaction}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf t\cdot\nabla\phi=0$, this entire vector is independent of the
velocity pair at the same spatial position, so $\Delta A\phi=0$.
Every such observable is conserved by the linear and nonlinear local
collision surrogates, regardless of the energy projector.

There is also a precise local necessity statement for spatial observables.
At a point with $s\ne0$ and $\mathbf t\ne0$, let
$\kappa=\mathbf t\cdot\nabla\phi$. Choose equal nonzero $u$ and unequal
$\mu,\mu'$ in a product velocity domain of nonzero widths. Their action
difference has only an $\eta$ component, while
$\xi_X=(\mu-\mu')\mathbf t/(qB)$ and
$\xi_\eta=u s(\mu-\mu')$. If $\kappa\ne0$, then
$\xi\cdot\Delta A\phi=-u s\kappa(\mu-\mu')^2/(qB)\ne0$ and the
spatial block of $P\Delta A\phi$ is nonzero. Continuity gives a positive
weak contribution on a neighborhood of this pair. Thus a spatial observable
is locally null precisely when $\kappa=0$ at such a point. If $s=0$, the
energy-flow difference is spatial, and the velocity-dependent part of
$\Delta A\phi$ is nonspatial; every spatial observable is locally null.
The case $\mathbf t=0$ already has $\kappa=0$. These statements classify
spatial test functions, not the full nonuniform nullspace.

Locally a vacuum field has $\mathbf B=\nabla\chi$. Since $\mathbf t$ is
orthogonal to both $\nabla B$ and $\nabla\chi$, every spatial $F(B,\chi)$
is a collision invariant. When $\chi$ is globally single valued, closed
collisions therefore preserve the joint spatial pushforward population
in $(B,\chi)$. The executed scalar potentials are
\begin{align}
 \chi_m&=Cz+a(z^3/3-zR^2/2),\\
 \chi_d&=-Cz/r^3,\qquad
 \chi_{na}=\chi_d+\varepsilon Cxy/L.
\end{align}
Ideal flow generally changes these populations. They are not interior
Casimirs of the Poisson tensor and are not claimed to be invariants of the
full combined dynamics.

\subsection*{Poloidal flux memory and inaccessible dipole candidates}
In an axisymmetric purely poloidal field, $\mathbf t$ is azimuthal. Therefore
all $\phi(R,z)$ satisfy the preceding sufficient condition: local
collisions preserve every meridional spatial population. With
$\mathbf B=\nabla\psi\times\mathbf e_\theta/R$, the poloidal fluxes are
\begin{equation}
 \psi_d=\frac{CR^2}{(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}},\qquad
 \psi_m=\frac{CR^2}{2}+\frac{aR^2z^2}{2}-\frac{aR^4}{8}.
 \label{eq:poloidalflux}
\end{equation}
Both $\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf t$ are tangent to these flux surfaces.
For constant potential, ideal flow conserves $\psi$; the same remains true
for an axisymmetric electrostatic potential. Under closed, flux-compatible
ideal boundaries and closed weak collision boundaries,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int F(\psi) f\,\dd\Gamma=0
 \quad\hbox{for every admissible smooth }F.
 \label{eq:fluxpopulation}
\end{equation}
This is conservation of the full flux pushforward marginal, which obstructs
accessibility of an energy/$G(\mu)$-only equilibrium from arbitrary initial
data. It does not generally preserve a joint $(\psi,\mu)$ population:
for example $h=\mu\psi$ has a nonzero projected pair action off the
equatorial plane. The test suite checks that counterexample explicitly.
In particular, under the same closed combined conditions, the stationary
family includes
\begin{equation}
 f=\exp[-\beta E+g(\mu)+F(\psi)]
 \label{eq:fluxstationaryfamily}
\end{equation}
whenever it is positive, normalizable and in the weak form domain.
The energy term is annihilated by $P$, the moment term by $\J$, and the flux
term has zero local pair difference; all three terms are ideal constants.
The earlier energy/moment candidate is therefore a proper subset of this
stationary family, not a complete equilibrium classification for this field
and closure. No completeness statement for \eqref{eq:fluxstationaryfamily}
is asserted.

Canonical toroidal momentum conservation and the ideal flux constraint in
the purely poloidal limit have established antecedents~\cite{TB15}.
Here $A\psi=(R\mathbf e_\theta/q,0,0)$ with the flux convention in
\eqref{eq:poloidalflux}; thus $q\psi$ is the lowest-order canonical rotation
generator. The additional conclusion is that exact spatial localization
makes \emph{every} $F(\psi)$ collisionally invariant. This proof uses equality
of the paired positions essentially. It does not apply to the generally
spatially nonlocal source Eq.~(119), to its full interaction tensor, or to
finite-Larmor collision displacements. The source introduces the spatial
scalar/delta approximation separately in Eqs.~(171)--(172).
Conventional guiding-center collision operators retain mechanisms absent
from this closure~\cite{B04,BS25}.
Flux- or canonical-momentum-dependent equilibria are not new objects:
Di Troia~\cite{DT15} treats equilibria depending on canonical toroidal
momentum, energy and moment, and modifies the Landau projection using the
selected equilibrium score difference in Eqs.~(44) and (46) of the checked
arXiv version. That target-dependent construction differs from a nullspace
constraint forced by the present fixed local pair geometry.
Sato, Yoshida and Kawazura~\cite{SYK15} construct inward diffusion from
fluctuating electric fields on a moment-preserving leaf; their Eq.~(14d)
contains a nonzero Wiener increment in $\psi$, and Eq.~(15) contains flux
diffusion. Such forcing changes the flux population and lies outside the
closed axisymmetric ideal-plus-local-collision assumptions here.
These antecedents preclude a broad novelty claim for constrained equilibria
or dipole transport. The present negative result concerns the specified
closure's inaccessible states, not a general prohibition of inward diffusion.
 Focused primary-literature searches found
no matching statement for this precise surrogate; no publication-priority
claim follows. Neither the invariant theorem nor the critical-point
counterexample provides a physical rate, a quantitative decay bound or a
dipole lifetime.

\subsection*{Symmetry breaking removes spatial ideal constants}
For the unit dipole plus $a\nabla(xy)$, $0<|a|\le0.03$, on the connected
Cartesian box stated above, there is no nonconstant $C^2$ spatial function
$\phi(\X)$ that is an ideal constant for every velocity state in an open
product domain. Consequently there is no nonconstant spatial function in
the common ideal/collision nullspace. This concerns spatial observables
only; it does not classify functions depending on $u,\mu$, establish
coercivity, or imply a relaxation rate.

Indeed, comparing the $\mu$ and $u$ coefficients of
$V_X\cdot\nabla\phi=0$ in \eqref{eq:vacuumflowmap} gives
$\mathbf b\cdot\nabla\phi=\mathbf t\cdot\nabla\phi=0$.
Set $S=|\mathbf B|^2$, $p=\mathbf B$, and
$v=\mathbf B\times\nabla S=2B^2\mathbf t$. Both vector fields annihilate
$\phi$, so their Lie bracket
$[p,v]=(\nabla v)p-(\nabla p)v$ also annihilates it.
At the interior unit-radius point $(15/17,0,8/17)$, exact rational
differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 \det[p,v,[p,v]]=
 \frac{900a(225a^2+481)(6709a^4-313674a^2+2686977)}{6975757441}.
 \label{eq:nonaxisrankcertificate}
\end{equation}
The quartic factor is at least
$2686977-313674(0.03)^2>2686694$ on the stated amplitude interval.
Thus the determinant is nonzero. The field is real analytic and bounded
away from zero throughout this box, and the analytic determinant is not
identically zero. Its nonzero set is therefore dense. On that set the
three annihilating vector fields span physical space, so $\nabla\phi=0$;
continuity and connectedness make $\phi$ constant throughout the box.
For $a=0.03$, the exact value is
$1396271742281072028423/279030297640000000000
 =5.004014811619193\ldots$; direct field automatic differentiation gives
$5.004014811619226$.

The certificate was derived independently by exact rational field
derivatives and by symbolic differentiation; regression tests check the
actual field implementation at both perturbation signs.
This two-vector Lie-rank argument is distinct from the one-field
Pfaff/Frobenius condition $\mathbf B\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf B=0$
discussed in SM24~\cite{SM24}. The latter holds for all these vacuum fields.
The result removes the axisymmetric spatial flux constraint, not all
phase-space constraints or all possible obstructions to mixing. Focused
primary-source searches found no matching statement for this fixed analytic
example, but no publication-priority claim is made.

\section{Linear weak discretization and SOLVAX}

Represent $h=\sum_i a_i\chi_i$. Define the mass and collision matrices by
\begin{align}
 M_{ij}&=\int f_0\chi_i\chi_j\dd\Gamma,\\
 K_{ij}&=\frac12\iint f_0f_0'\Delta\chi_i^T\Pi_0\Delta\chi_j
 \dd\Gamma\dd\Gamma'.
 \label{eq:gram}
\end{align}
Positive quadrature gives a Gram form $K=B_p^TW_pB_p\succeq0$. Implement its
action without assembling the full pair tensor. A dense assembly on a tiny grid
must remain an independent reference.

The executable nodal construction uses tensor differentiation followed by
$A_i\phi=T_i\J_{\mu,i}(\nabla_h\phi)_i$ in a common Cartesian $\eta$ chart.
For every unordered pair $(i,j)$, set $B_{ij}\phi=A_i\phi-A_j\phi$ and construct
$\xi_{ij}=B_{ij}e$ from the same discrete energy vector $e$ and derivative.
The nonuniform projector uses this discrete $\xi$, rather than inserting an
incompatible continuum energy gradient. This is an explicitly stated discrete
energy projector; its consistency must be checked under refinement.
The scalar pair weight is $w_X(Bw_v)_i(Bw_v)_j$ locally and
$w_iw_jW_{ij}$ for a spatially nonlocal model. In a toroidal chart the spatial
weight contains $R\dd R\dd\theta\dd z$ while each local velocity factor contains
$B\dd u\dd\mu$, even though the one-particle product $BR=C$ simplifies.

The ordered weak form in \eqref{eq:gram} counts $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$ equally:
exchange reverses both pair differences, leaving their quadratic product
unchanged. Therefore its $1/2$ exactly cancels the two ordered contributions,
and each unordered pair is inserted once with no $1/2$. Self-node pair
differences are identically zero and need no stored contribution. Distinct
nodes with equal energy-flow vectors are a separate issue: the uniform field
uses its derived continuous limit, while an exactly zero nonuniform pair vector
is rejected. No $|\xi|^2+\epsilon$ is used. Directional limits may depend on the
approach. The continuous local zero set is classified by
\eqref{eq:vacuumflowmap}; the critical-point counterexample
\eqref{eq:criticalweaklimit} prevents a universal continuous prescription.
Successful finite-grid examples do not by themselves establish a valid
extension for a modified discrete energy map.

Periodic derivatives are Fourier derivatives; the real even-grid Nyquist
component is unresolved and given zero derivative. Nonperiodic derivatives
support local three-point quadratic interpolation and, for the refined
nonuniform boxes, the derivative of the full nodal Lagrange polynomial.
The derivative and its transpose enter the weak collision assembly.
The selected spatial quadrature and derivative method are recorded explicitly.
This imposes the natural collision flux rather than
wrapping nonperiodic field values. The preassembled reference path retains
pair indices and directions with quadratic velocity-pair storage (and spatial
pairs for nonlocal models). The compact local path instead stores energy flows
and forms pair directions only inside bounded blocks, as described below.

If $c$ represents a collision invariant and the derivative/kernel construction
respects it, then $Kc=0$. Include the discrete energy and all resolved
magnetic-moment-bin constraints. Finite differences do not automatically obey
the product rules needed for an arbitrary sampled $\mu B$. Use the same discrete
energy gradient in the degeneracy direction and in the weak operator, and prove
consistency as the mesh is refined.

The collision-only system and norm balance are
\begin{equation}
 M\dot a=-Ka,\qquad
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\frac12a^TMa=-a^TKa\le0.
 \label{eq:semidiscrete}
\end{equation}
With a compatible stationary Hamiltonian discretization,
\begin{equation}
 M\dot a=(A-K)a,\qquad A^T=-A.
 \label{eq:combined}
\end{equation}
Also test $Ac=0$ for the required linear invariants; skew symmetry alone does not
imply those individual identities.

In the nonuniform combined example, exact Fourier half-translations in the
periodic toroidal angle and $z$ surround one backward-Euler collision step.
The translation uses $\dot\theta=u/R$ and
$\dot z=(m u^2+\mu C/R)/(qC)$ at each fixed $(R,u,\mu)$.
The measure $BR=C$, energy and moment marginal are invariant under this ideal
flow, and radial walls are tangent. This symmetric composition remains first
order because the collision substep is backward Euler. It is a linear,
fixed-equilibrium combined evolution; the nonlinear examples are collision only.

A backward-Euler collision step solves
\begin{equation}
 (M+\Delta tK)a^{n+1}=Ma^n.
 \label{eq:backward}
\end{equation}
For positive-definite $M$, this is an appropriate PCG problem. SOLVAX's
\texttt{pcg\_linear\_solve} supplies a converged-system differentiation wrapper
\cite{SOLVAX}. Do not pass $M^{-1}K$ to Euclidean PCG without mass scaling or a
proof of symmetry. A coupled implicit step with $A$ is generally nonsymmetric
and calls for GMRES or another suitable method.

For a linear invariant $c$, multiply \eqref{eq:backward} by $c^T$ to check its
conservation. Backward Euler also satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Q^{n+1}-Q^n=-\Delta t(a^{n+1})^TKa^{n+1}
 -\frac12\norm{a^{n+1}-a^n}_M^2\le0.
\end{equation}
This does not prove positivity of a reconstructed finite-amplitude distribution.
An implicit midpoint step can be second order, but is not automatically a good
stiff-decay scheme or a nonlinear entropy-preserving integrator.

Local pair application costs $O(N_XN_v^2)$ in a direct quadrature. A spatially
nonlocal direct application can cost $O(N_X^2N_v^2)$. Chunk pair contractions,
reuse geometry and quadrature, and keep derivatives sparse. Block-tridiagonal
solves may be useful in a line preconditioner; the full velocity coupling is not
generally tridiagonal.
The current tensor derivative matrices are stored densely, including zeros
outside the nonperiodic stencils. Their contraction adds
$O(N\sum_\alpha n_\alpha)$ work, with $N=\prod_\alpha n_\alpha$; stencil
sparsity is a mathematical property, not an implemented sparse matmul claim.

\section{A nonlinear discrete entropy construction}

This finite-dimensional fixed-field construction is implemented. It is not a
guarantee for arbitrary interpolation, self-consistent fields, or nonlinear
Hamiltonian transport. It follows the weak-structure approach developed for
Landau discretizations \cite{KH17,HBK18,H21,ZPH}.

Let $f_i>0$, $w_i>0$, and $n_i=w_if_i$. Construct a symmetric positive mobility
$K(f)$ from the discrete pair weak form, with
\begin{equation}
 K\bm1=0,\quad Ke=0,\quad Kc_\mu=0,
 \label{eq:discreteconstraints}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is the fixed-field energy vector and $c_\mu$ spans the represented
moment-marginal constraints. Define
\begin{equation}
 S(n)=-\sum_i n_i\ln(n_i/w_i).
\end{equation}
Then the collision ODE can be written
\begin{equation}
 \dot n=K(f)\nabla_nS=-K(f)\ln f.
 \label{eq:nonlinearode}
\end{equation}
The constant term in $\nabla_nS=-\ln f-\bm1$ vanishes through
\eqref{eq:discreteconstraints}. Consequently $\dot S\ge0$ and
$\dd(e^Tn)/\dd t=0$. This is an algebraic guarantee only for the actual
constructed $K$.

For each component define a divided difference of $s_i(n)=-n\ln(n/w_i)$:
\begin{equation}
 (\overline\nabla S)_i=
 \begin{cases}
 [s_i(n_i^+)-s_i(n_i^-)]/(n_i^+-n_i^-),&n_i^+\ne n_i^-,\\
 -\ln(n_i^-/w_i)-1,&n_i^+=n_i^-.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:discretegradient}
\end{equation}
Evaluate it stably near equal arguments using logarithmic divided differences,
not cancellation-prone subtraction. A step
\begin{equation}
 n^+-n^-=\Delta t\,\overline K\,\overline\nabla S,
 \qquad \overline K=\overline K^T\succeq0
 \label{eq:discretestep}
\end{equation}
with the null directions in \eqref{eq:discreteconstraints} gives
\begin{equation}
 S(n^+)-S(n^-)=\Delta t\,
 (\overline\nabla S)^T\overline K\overline\nabla S\ge0.
\end{equation}
The conservation laws follow by left multiplication with each invariant vector.
Use a positive mean state to construct $\overline K$ and a converged nonlinear
solve. Parameterizing $n_i^+=w_i e^{g_i^+}$ enforces positive nodal populations,
but does not prove existence of the solve or positivity between interpolation
nodes. Check all required quadrature/reconstruction locations.

\subsection*{Matrix-free Newton--GMRES and residual control}
The compact nonuniform evolution solves the same discrete-gradient equation
as the small dense reference. Let $l=\ln f^+$, $l^-=\ln f^-$,
$\bar f=(e^l+e^{l^-})/2$, and write the unscaled residual as
\begin{equation}
 F(l)=w\,(e^l-f^-)-\Delta t K(\bar f)\overline\nabla S(l^-,l).
 \label{eq:dgrootresidual}
\end{equation}
Products with $w$ are componentwise. Set $a_i=\sqrt{w_i f_i^-}>0$,
$r=F/a$, and parameterize the Newton correction as $\delta l=v/a$.
The exact matrix-free operator is
\begin{equation}
 v\longmapsto a^{-1}J_F(l)(v/a),\qquad
 a^{-1}J_F(l)(v/a)=-r.
 \label{eq:scalednewton}
\end{equation}
For the compact path, the pair tangent is prepared exactly. If
$\mathcal A h$ is the nodal common-chart action, a pair contribution to
$K(\bar f)h$ is proportional to
$\bar f_i\bar f_j P I_XP\Delta\mathcal A h$. Its variation is
\begin{equation}
 \delta[K(\bar f)h]=\mathcal A^T\sum_{ij}
 D w_Xv_iv_j\bar f_i\bar f_j P I_XP
 \left[\Delta\mathcal A\delta h
       +(r_i+r_j)\Delta\mathcal A h\right],\qquad
 r_i=\frac{f_i^+}{f_i^++f_i^-}\delta l_i.
 \label{eq:preparedpairtangent}
\end{equation}
The sum denotes the same paired flux accumulation as the mobility, including
the local velocity Jacobians $v_i$. Here $h=\overline\nabla S$ and its
componentwise derivative gives $\delta h=h'_i\delta l_i$.
The actions $\mathcal A h$, nodal mean populations and scalar $h'_i$ are
cached once per Newton iterate; each Krylov matvec applies one projected pair
tangent. No frozen-mobility approximation is made. Independent forward
Jacobian-vector products on the explicit-pair path differentiate all terms
and check this prepared tangent. SOLVAX restarted GMRES solves this system; a finite-step
Newton Jacobian need not be symmetric positive definite, so PCG is not
substituted. Neither route retains a linearization tape containing every
primal pair block.

The nonlinear relative stopping norm is
$\|F/a\|_2/\sqrt{\sum_i w_if_i^-}$, fixed by the previous state.
The dense reference retains its original population residual norm; it solves
the same unscaled equation with its own explicitly recorded tolerance.
A backtracking line search decreases the chosen residual norm and rejects
nonfinite or exponential-underflow candidates. It clips no population and
applies no invariant correction.
Since the divided difference gives
$\Delta S=\overline\nabla S\cdot w(f^+-f^-)$, the exact entropy defect is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta S-\Delta t\overline\nabla S^TK(\bar f)\overline\nabla S
 =\overline\nabla S\cdot F,
 \qquad
 |\mathrm{defect}|\le
 \|a\,\overline\nabla S\|_2\|F/a\|_2.
 \label{eq:entropyresidualbound}
\end{equation}
The implementation checks this bound with a floating-point allowance.
Likewise, if $Kc=0$, the invariant error is $c\cdot F$ and is bounded by
$\|ac\|_2\|F/a\|_2$. These are residual-dependent bounds, not exact
conservation assertions for an unconverged root.

For the compact local Cartesian grid the stored coefficients are
$N\times5\times4$, and energy flows, populations, moment indices and local
quadrature have $O(N)$ entries. Dense axis derivative matrices add
$O(\sum_\alpha n_\alpha^2)$ storage. No local pair list or dense Newton
Jacobian is stored. A target-row contraction processes all $N_v$ partners;
with pair budget $C_p$, temporary pair workspace is
$O(\max(C_p,N_v))$ fixed-dimensional vectors. Restarted GMRES additionally
stores $O(Nr)$ basis entries for restart length $r$.
Thus the precise storage bound is
$O(N+\sum_\alpha n_\alpha^2+Nr+\max(C_p,N_v))$, apart from compiler/runtime
storage. Measured grid bytes and process timings are reported independently.
This does not supply an accelerated pair asymptotic: the direct local
contraction still evaluates $O(N_XN_v^2)$ interactions per matvec.

For the executed nonlinear uniform and linear toroidal examples, continuous
spatial Fourier positivity is checked with a sufficient certificate. If
$f(x)=\sum_k\widehat f_k e^{ikx}$, then
\begin{equation}
 f(x)\ge\widehat f_0-\sum_{k\ne0}|\widehat f_k|.
\end{equation}
The same triangle bound holds for a double Fourier sum in $(\theta,z)$.
The example rejects a nonpositive bound, and additionally oversamples the
reconstruction. Positive piecewise-linear interpolation in the remaining
coordinates then preserves positivity throughout that declared reconstruction.
This certificate is conditional on the computed coefficients and is not a
positivity theorem for arbitrary Fourier states or higher-order velocity
interpolation. The collision derivative still uses its explicitly stated local
quadratic differentiation, independently of the positivity-preserving display
reconstruction. The Cartesian collision-only boxes use positive nodal values
with piecewise-linear reconstruction.

For self-consistent fields the energy is nonlinear in $n$. Its discrete gradient
must enter the collision degeneracy consistently; the fixed vector $e$ above is
not enough. Likewise, nonlinear Hamiltonian transport requires its own
conservative and positivity-compatible treatment. A linear skew matrix alone
is not a proof of a nonlinear H-theorem.

\section{Scattering physics and validity tests}

The constrained model assumes negligible accumulated change of magnetic moment.
That assumption is stronger than a small collision frequency compared with the
gyrofrequency. At constant $B$, an energy change satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Delta E=\frac{m}{2}\Delta(u^2)+B\Delta\mu+q\Delta\Phi.
 \label{eq:energychange}
\end{equation}
Small $\Delta E$ does not separately bound $\Delta\mu$.
In particular, the approximate identification in SM25 (85), printed p.~10,
requires an extra bound on parallel-energy exchange: at constant $B$,
\begin{equation}
 B|\Delta\mu|\le |\Delta E|+
 \tfrac12m|\Delta(u^2)|+|q\Delta\Phi|.
\end{equation}
Spatial locality and nearly constant $B$ alone supply no bound on the second
term. This audit concerns the inference from small energy change; it does not
rule out a stronger regime in which every relevant exchange is ordered small. For independent unbiased
increments, zero mean accumulation can coexist with
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{j=1}^N\delta\mu_j\right)
 =N\operatorname{Var}(\delta\mu).
\end{equation}
For independent increments, both
$N|\langle\delta\mu\rangle|/\mu_{\rm ref}\ll1$ and
$N\operatorname{Var}(\delta\mu)/\mu_{\rm ref}^2\ll1$ are needed for small accumulated
change on a chosen nonzero scale. Compensating mean changes can still alter the
full moment marginal through diffusion. These are audit points for SM25
(85)--(86), which the source itself calls working assumptions, rather than
replacements for its ordering.

A controlled microscopic reference can integrate
\begin{equation}
 m_s\dot{\bm v}_s=q_s\bm v_s\times\bm B-\nabla_{\bm r_s}V,
 \qquad \dot{\bm r}_s=\bm v_s,
 \label{eq:encounter}
\end{equation}
with a specified screened potential, for example
$V(r)=q_1q_2e^{-r/\lambda}/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)$. Screening is a declared model
choice, not a result of this two-body calculation. Resolve close encounters or
exclude them with a stated grazing criterion. The generalized magnetized
scattering theory in Ref.~\cite{JB20} is an important reference.

For uniform $\bm B$, compare with the unperturbed helical orbit at identical
incoming conditions. Use
\begin{equation}
 \bm R_s=\bm r_s+\frac{\bm v_s\times\bm b}{\Omega_s},\qquad
 \mu_s=\frac{m_s|\bm v_{\perp s}|^2}{2B},\quad \Omega_s=q_sB/m_s.
\end{equation}
Extract asymptotic incoming/outgoing guiding-center data at a common reference
time. A raw endpoint displacement can contain an arbitrary flight-time
contribution after the encounter changes parallel velocity.

Sample incident flux, impact parameters, gyrophases and relative velocities with
the appropriate measure. Check timestep, start/end distance, angular sampling and
screening/cutoff convergence independently. Recover a Rutherford limit before
using strong-field results. Attractive close encounters outside the grazing
regime are not automatically described by the reduced kinetic closure.

For independent encounters at a stated rate $\nu_{\rm enc}$, diagnostic drift and
diffusion coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 A_\mu=\nu_{\rm enc}\langle\delta\mu\rangle,\qquad
 D_{\mu\mu}=\frac{\nu_{\rm enc}}2\langle(\delta\mu)^2\rangle.
 \label{eq:driftdiffusion}
\end{equation}
Use both, together with cross-covariances and correlation tests. A timescale
estimate must specify a nonzero population scale $\mu_{\rm ref}$; the quantities
$\mu_{\rm ref}/|A_\mu|$ and $\mu_{\rm ref}^2/(2D_{\mu\mu})$ diagnose different
failures of the constraint. Near $\mu=0$, use a suitable distribution-level metric.

Compare these with an independently determined mixing time. A physically
supported constrained interval requires
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\rm mix}\ll t\ll\tau_\mu.
\end{equation}
Choosing a small input pitch-angle rate establishes a synthetic asymptotic test,
not this physical inequality. Adding two collision terms also requires a
nonoverlapping process decomposition; it can otherwise double count encounters.

\section{Independent homogeneous controls and encounters}

\subsection*{Lorentz pitch-angle evolution}

For a fixed physical speed shell, the implemented operator is
$C_{\rm Lz}=\nu\partial_\xi[(1-\xi^2)\partial_\xi]/2$, with natural endpoint
flux at $\xi=\pm1$. The Legendre equation gives eigenrates
$\nu\ell(\ell+1)/2$. Gauss--Legendre projection and exact modal exponentials
provide an independent solution; the tests additionally apply the differential
operator to Legendre polynomials. Thus the $\ell=0,1,2,3$ rates are
$0,\nu,3\nu,6\nu$. Shell population and energy are preserved, while the
$\ell=1$ momentum is transferred to the fixed reservoir.
The coordinate map and full Jacobian are
\begin{equation}
 u=v\xi,\quad \mu=\frac{m v^2(1-\xi^2)}{2B},\quad
 \left|\frac{\partial(u,\mu)}{\partial(v,\xi)}\right|=\frac{m v^2}{B},\quad
 \dd^3v=\frac{2\pi B}{m}\dd u\dd\mu.
\end{equation}
The shell comparison changes $\mu$ while preserving speed, as ordinary pitch
scattering must; it is not an unbalanced bare moment diffusion term.

\subsection*{Nonlinear conserving Dougherty evolution}

The physical three-velocity operator is
\begin{equation}
 C_D[F]=\nu\nabla_v\cdot[(\bm v-\bm U)F+\theta\nabla_vF],\quad
 \bm U=\langle\bm v\rangle,\quad
 \theta=\tfrac13\langle|\bm v-\bm U|^2\rangle.
\end{equation}
Integration against $1$, $\bm v$ and $|\bm v-\bm U|^2$ proves that $\bm U$ and
$\theta$ are constant for closed homogeneous evolution. For a positive
normalized Gaussian mixture with weights $p_a$, component means $\bm U_a$ and
covariances $A_a$, the exact solution remains a positive mixture with
\begin{equation}
 \bm U_a(t)=\bm U+e^{-\nu t}(\bm U_a(0)-\bm U),\quad
 A_a(t)=e^{-2\nu t}A_a(0)+\theta(1-e^{-2\nu t})I.
\end{equation}
Substituting each Gaussian into the now constant-moment Fokker--Planck equation
verifies this solution; conservation makes it a solution of the original
nonlinear model. It includes a non-Gaussian control, without substituting
prescribed reservoir moments. Exact Gaussian gradients and Laplacians evaluate
the strong operator independently of a centered temporal difference.

In \texttt{03\_controls.py}, Lorentz modal errors are below $6.5\times10^{-15}$.
The two-component Dougherty mixture has nonzero drift and anisotropic
covariance. A $40^3$ physical Cartesian velocity quadrature on extent 9 gives
particle error $2.0\times10^{-11}$, covariance error $7.82\times10^{-10}$ and
centered-energy error $4.76\times10^{-10}$. The strong equation versus centered
time derivative error is $2.21\times10^{-10}$. The sampled entropy increases;
positive mixture reconstruction is analytic, and the smallest sampled density
is $1.96\times10^{-64}$.

\subsection*{Physical three-velocity Landau weak reference}

The conventional one-species normalization used here is
\begin{equation}
 C[F]=\Gamma\nabla_v\cdot\int FF' U(\bm w)
       [\nabla_v\ln F-\nabla_{v'}\ln F']\dd^3v',\quad
 U(\bm w)=\frac{I-\hat{\bm w}\hat{\bm w}^{T}}{|\bm w|},\quad
 \bm w=\bm v-\bm v'.
 \label{eq:landaucontrol}
\end{equation}
With $\int F\dd^3v=n$, the SI coefficient is
$\Gamma=q^4\ln\Lambda/(8\pi\epsilon_0^2m^2)$, matching the mass convention of
Fitzpatrick's Eqs.~(3.93)--(3.94) \cite{FitzpatrickLandau}.
Writing $F=(n/v_*^3)\widehat F$ gives the frequency scale
$\Gamma n/v_*^3$. The Coulomb logarithm and its cutoffs are supplied parameters;
this conversion does not calibrate the constrained scalar $D$.

For a normalized Gaussian of covariance $A$, $\bm w$ has covariance $2A$ and
the pair score is $\bm g=-A^{-1}\bm w$. The exact weak covariance and entropy
rates are
\begin{align}
 \dot A&=-\frac\Gamma2\mathbb E[
   \bm w\otimes U\bm g+U\bm g\otimes\bm w],\\
 \dot S&=\frac\Gamma2\mathbb E[\bm g\cdot U\bm g]\ge0.
\end{align}
Trace conservation follows pointwise from $U\bm w=0$.
The center velocity integrates analytically; radial/polar/gyrophase quadrature
retains the full physical $\dd^3w$ geometry. A separate Cartesian Gauss--Hermite
expectation avoids $\bm w=0$ by even quadrature order.
A third independent check diagonalizes $A$ and uses the Laplace integrals for
$1/r$ and $1/r^3$. Define
$Z(t)=\prod_j(1+4A_jt)^{-1/2}$ and $V_j(t)=2A_j/(1+4A_jt)$; then
\begin{equation}
 \dot A_i=\frac\Gamma{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty Z(t)
 \left[t^{-1/2}\frac{V_i}{A_i}
 -2t^{1/2}V_i\sum_j\frac{(1+2\delta_{ij})V_j}{A_j}\right]\dd t.
 \label{eq:landaulaplace}
\end{equation}
Gaussian second and fourth moments after multiplication by $e^{-tr^2}$ give
$V_i$ and $(1+2\delta_{ij})V_iV_j$, proving the integrand.
The substitution $t=s^2$ removes its endpoint singularity before adaptive
one-dimensional integration.

For $A=\diag(1.15,1.15,0.7)$ and $\Gamma=1$, the covariance derivatives are
\[(-0.0740393,-0.0740393,0.148079),\qquad \dot S=0.0413884.\]
The reported energy
rate residual is $2.1\times10^{-18}$. Radial quadrature refinement reaches a
$4.14\times10^{-14}$ discrepancy from \eqref{eq:landaulaplace} at order 28,
while the independent Cartesian Hermite route converges more slowly from
$8.83\%$ at order 8 to $0.316\%$ at order 48. Tail and denominator-softening
scans are saved separately. Softening changes only $1/r$ and retains $U\bm w=0$;
it is a convergence diagnostic, not an unmodified Coulomb tensor.
A small-anisotropy check takes $A=I+\epsilon\diag(1,1,-2)$.
At first order, $\mathbb E|\bm w|=4/\sqrt\pi$ for covariance $2I$ and the
angular moments are
$\mathbb E[n_i^2]=1/3$ and
$\mathbb E[n_i^2n_j^2]=(1+2\delta_{ij})/15$.
Substitution in the weak covariance rate gives
$\dot A_i=-4\Gamma\epsilon D_i/(5\sqrt\pi)+O(\epsilon^2)$,
where $D=(1,1,-2)$. The instantaneous normalized stress rate approaches
$4\Gamma/(5\sqrt\pi)=0.451351667$; for $\epsilon=0.03,0.01,0.003$ the two
independent references agree and give $0.457605,0.453335,0.451936$ respectively.
This is a limiting conventional control, without a discovery claim.

Maxwellian score differences are parallel to $\bm w$ and annihilate the kernel
for multiple drifts and temperatures. A separate positive non-Gaussian nodal
pair calculation checks weak number/momentum/energy conservation and positive
entropy production. Its omitted coincident-node contribution is a stated
quadrature prescription, not a general directional limit. These controls
verify instantaneous weak moments; no full nonlinear Landau kinetic time
integration or Gaussian closure trajectory is claimed.

\subsection*{Direct screened encounter audit}

\texttt{04\_encounters.py} directly integrates equal-mass, equal-charge repulsive
pairs in a uniform field with $V(r)=\kappa e^{-r/\lambda}/r$. It measures changes
relative to the free helix at identical incoming conditions. Endpoint refinement
holds fixed the incoming guiding center and phase at the $z=0$ crossing;
changing the initial plane at fixed raw position and phase would instead
change the orbit and is not a valid endpoint convergence test.
For relative guiding-center offset $b$, $v_\parallel$ and gyrophase $\varphi$
at that crossing, the incoming plane $z=-L$ has phase
$\varphi+\Omega L/v_\parallel$ and transverse position
$(b-v_y/\Omega,v_x/\Omega)$. This follows directly from the invariant free
$\bm R=\bm r+\bm v\times\bm b/\Omega$.

A documented case uses $b=1.2$, $v_\parallel=1$, $v_\perp=0.35$,
$\Omega=2$, $\kappa=0.05$, $\lambda=4$ and 32 phases.
Increasing $L$ from 12 to 32 changes the mean moment increment from
$5.0126\times10^{-5}$ to $5.0074\times10^{-5}$ and its second moment from
$9.4135\times10^{-7}$ to $9.4059\times10^{-7}$.
The individual incoming $\mu$ is $0.00765625$, so the rms increment is
approximately $12.7\%$ of that scale while the ensemble pair-energy error is below $3.4\times10^{-11}$.
This is a controlled counterexample to inferring moment preservation
solely from energy conservation; it does not characterize every weak encounter
regime. For the same $B=2$ case, the maximum relative velocity change from the free
helix is $0.0340$, the minimum sampled separation is $1.045$, and
$\rho/\lambda=0.0219$, $\rho/b_{90}\simeq0.982$,
$\Omega\lambda/v_{\rm rel}\simeq7.55$. Uniform $B$ gives $\rho/L_B=0$;
$\nu/\Omega$ is unassigned because no encounter rate is measured.
The free-orbit control has moment drift below $1.2\times10^{-14}$ and guiding
center drift below $6\times10^{-16}$. In a weak-field unscreened Rutherford
control, increasing endpoint distance from 12 to 96 reduces the angular error,
but the final relative discrepancy remains $1.11\times10^{-3}$ and is recorded
as finite-endpoint error. A separate close-encounter timestep scan decreases
pair-energy error from $5.40\times10^{-4}$ to $3.57\times10^{-9}$.
The two individual moment increments are correlated by the imposed symmetric
center-of-mass initialization; saved joint covariances must not be interpreted
as independent species statistics.

No incident-flux/impact-parameter ensemble, plasma encounter frequency,
or independently measured lifetime is assigned to these phase statistics.

\section{Research questions and evidence}

\textbf{Geometry and nullspace.} Begin with the proven finite-quadrature spectrum
\eqref{eq:uniformspectrum}. Track its changes under a valid mirror, toroidal or
dipole field. Separate collision null modes from decay generated through coupling
to Hamiltonian transport. A small positive eigenvalue on one finite grid is not a
continuum gap. Vary all resolutions, domains and velocity tails.

\textbf{Localization.} Compare the local kernel with a specified symmetric
finite-range kernel, using a derived normalization as the range shrinks.
Boundary renormalization must retain pair symmetry. A Gaussian-range surrogate
remains a surrogate unless independent scattering physics supports it.

\textbf{Constrained dipole states.} Ref.~\cite{S23} studies maximum-entropy dipole
states and additional adiabatic constraints. Here the result sought is a dynamical
relaxation law, an extra invariant, a loss of mixing, or an independently measured
lifetime. Reproducing \eqref{eq:density} is verification rather than discovery.

For any geometry-amplitude scaling, compare competing fits and inspect projector
degeneracies before using regular perturbation theory. For steady-state parameter
derivatives specify which particle, energy and marginal constraints are held

\subsection*{Bounded Maxwellian encounter flux and thermal center motion}
Let $\theta=k_BT/m$ denote a single-particle velocity variance. For two equal
Maxwellian particles, $\bm w=\bm v_1-\bm v_2$ and
$\bm V=(\bm v_1+\bm v_2)/2$ are independent, with covariances
$2\theta I$ and $\theta I/2$. The transformation has absolute Jacobian one.
The relative distribution and equations for a screened pair potential are
\begin{align}
 F_{\rm rel}(\bm w)&=(4\pi\theta)^{-3/2}e^{-|\bm w|^2/(4\theta)},\\
 \dot{\bm r}&=\bm w,\qquad
 \dot{\bm w}=\Omega\bm w\times\bm e_z+
 \frac{2\kappa}{m}\frac{e^{-r/L_s}(1+r/L_s)}{r^3}\bm r,
 \qquad \Omega=qB/m.
\end{align}
The conserved relative energy is $m|\bm w|^2/4+\kappa e^{-r/L_s}/r$;
center motion follows an independent free helix. The new six-dimensional
relative integrator is checked against the independent twelve-dimensional
pair integrator, including nonunit masses and both charge signs.

For guiding-center impact $b$, the translation to the incoming perpendicular
position has unit Jacobian. Axisymmetry integrates its orientation to
$2\pi b\,\dd b$. On a plane normal to the field, the two-sided incoming
crossing measure per partner density is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\nu}{n}=2(2\pi b\,\dd b)\,w_\parallel F_{\rm rel}(\bm w)
 w_\perp\,\dd w_\perp\,\dd w_\parallel\,\dd\varphi,
 \qquad w_\parallel>0.
\end{equation}
The factor two includes the opposite incoming direction. There is no
unordered-pair half factor for this tagged-particle flux, and the plane flux
uses $|w_\parallel|$, not the cross-section flux $|\bm w|$.
For impact area $A=\pi(b_{\max}^2-b_{\min}^2)$, the bounded integral is
$2A(2\pi)J_\parallel J_\perp/(4\pi\theta)^{3/2}$, where
$J(a,b)=2\theta[e^{-a^2/(4\theta)}-e^{-b^2/(4\theta)}]$.
This supplies an independent analytic normalization.

Undo outgoing free gyromotion when defining
$\Delta\bm w_\perp=\overline{\bm w}_{\rm out,\perp}-\bm w_{\rm in,\perp}$.
With $c=m\Delta|\bm w_\perp|^2/(8B)$, the individual changes are
$\Delta\mu_{1,2}=c\pm[m/(2B)]\bm V_\perp\cdot\Delta\bm w_\perp$.
Exact Gaussian center averaging therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \langle\Delta\mu_1\rangle_V&=c,\\
 \langle(\Delta\mu_1)^2\rangle_V&=c^2+\sigma_c^2,
 &\langle\Delta\mu_1\Delta\mu_2\rangle_V&=c^2-\sigma_c^2,\\
 \sigma_c^2&=\frac{m^2\theta}{8B^2}|\Delta\bm w_\perp|^2.
\end{align}
A separate Gaussian quadrature of the actual individual initial/final
velocities verifies these raw moments. A zero-center trajectory omits
$\sigma_c^2$. The drift and diffusion moment integrals are
$A_\mu/n=\int\langle\Delta\mu\rangle_V\dd\nu/n$ and
$D_{\mu\mu}/n=\tfrac12\int\langle(\Delta\mu)^2\rangle_V\dd\nu/n$;
the latter half is the second-moment convention, not pair counting.

The executed normalized case uses $m=q=1$, $B=2$, $\kappa=0.03$, $L_s=3$,
$\theta=0.5$, $b\in[1.2,2.4]$, $w_\parallel\in[0.7,1.5]$ and
$w_\perp\in[0.2,0.8]$. The $4^3\times16$ base quadrature is independently
refined in each impact/speed coordinate through orders $3,4,5$, in gyrophase
through $8,16,32$, and by a half-grid phase shift. Moving the planes through
$L=18,24,32$ preserves the same incoming free helix. The first outward exit
is recorded; reflected trajectories are retained, and failure to exit within
$6L/w_\parallel$ raises. Eight held-out states additionally check active maximum
steps $0.2,0.1,0.05$. Direct quadrature changes are below $0.021\%$;
analytic flux normalization error is $1.62\times10^{-9}$.
The bounded results are $\nu/n=1.2601061$,
$A_\mu/n=3.11546\times10^{-6}$ and
$D_{\mu\mu}/n=8.00765\times10^{-7}$ in the stated normalized units.

A supplementary multilinear scattering table is tested on 64 uniformly
sampled states in the inner quadrature-node hull (seed 701).
Its held-out second-moment relative RMS error is $20.5\%$, exceeding the
predeclared $10\%$ target; this surrogate remains unresolved despite converged
direct integrals. These speed bands contain only $11.6\%$ of the full thermal
incoming flux in the same annulus. Omitted impact/speed regions and successive
encounter correlations remain untested. Truncation can exclude reverse
encounters, so the positive conditional drift is not a full Maxwellian-plasma
drift. Neither these bounded crossing coefficients nor the interpolation
checks calibrate the constrained scalar $D$ or establish a dipole lifetime.

For a repulsive uniform-field pair,
\begin{equation}
 \ddot z=k(t)z,\qquad
 k(t)=\frac{2\kappa}{m}\frac{e^{-r/L_s}(1+r/L_s)}{r^3}\ge0.
 \label{eq:encounterparallelturn}
\end{equation}
On $z<0$, the incoming positive parallel velocity decreases monotonically.
If it turns, the particle thereafter recedes toward the incoming exit and
cannot turn again. If it crosses $z=0$ with positive velocity, that velocity
increases on $z>0$. Reaching $(z,\dot z)=(0,0)$ at a finite time is excluded
by uniqueness of the regular equations unless the trajectory stays in that
plane. Thus an exiting trajectory has at most one parallel turn. A finite
flight-budget failure can involve slow outward travel or long near-turning
motion; it does not establish a bound pair or a failure of molecular chaos.

successive-encounter correlations remain unchecked. No physical constrained
coefficient, plasma relaxation time or dipole lifetime follows.

\subsection*{Continuous local geometric sensitivity}
Example 14 differentiates an initial local entropy-production density, not
a kinetic trajectory or an implicitly defined steady state. Its prescribed
distribution is
\begin{equation}
 \ln f=-E-0.2\mu+p,\qquad
 p=0.1\sin(x)u+0.04y\mu.
\end{equation}
At $\X=(1,0.12,0.3)$, $m=1.3$, $q=-0.8$, $D=0.1$, define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P(\X)=\frac{DB^2}{2}
 \iint ff'\left|I_XP\Delta A\ln f\right|^2
 \dd u\dd\mu\dd u'\dd\mu'.
 \label{eq:localproductionderivative}
\end{equation}
Energy and moment degeneracy give
$P\Delta A\ln f=P\Delta Ap$. The independent NumPy reference reconstructs
$A p$ from the analytic gradient
$\nabla_Xp=(0.1\cos(x)u,0.04\mu,0)$, $p_u=0.1\sin(x)$ and the vacuum
flow \eqref{eq:vacuumflowmap}; it shares only the field derivative data.
Both evaluations retain the two local $B$ Jacobians. Automatic
differentiation translates the sample in $x$ while holding the analytic
distribution definition, velocity bounds and field parameters fixed; hence
it differentiates $B^2$, $f,f'$, both observable actions and the energy-flow
projector. No part of the geometry is stopped in this derivative.

On the base $25\times21$ velocity quadrature, $|u|\le4$, $0\le\mu\le20$,
the mirror, dipole and perturbed dipole respectively give
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal P,\partial_x\mathcal P)_m
   &=(0.00142987874,-0.00316286298),\\
 (\mathcal P,\partial_x\mathcal P)_d
   &=(0.00528569785,0.0324274535),\\
 (\mathcal P,\partial_x\mathcal P)_{na}
   &=(0.00507509906,0.0310717576).
\end{align}
The independent pair values differ by at most $1.03\times10^{-15}$ relative.
Sixteen independent centered-difference steps from $0.04$ to $10^{-8}$
show the derivative plateau; the last-three-step maximum relative discrepancy
is below $1.54\times10^{-8}$. Separate velocity-order checks, expanded
$|u|\le5$ and $\mu\le24$ bounds, and quadrature refinement on each expanded
domain give final derivative changes below $0.661\%$.
The local maps are injective on these domains, as established above.
This is a checked derivative of the specified normalized weak observable,
with no general steady-state sensitivity or physical-rate inference.

\subsection*{Positive dipole distributions with incompatible flux populations}
Example 15 constructs a finite-domain counterexample without a time solve.
Use poloidal flux $\psi$, magnetic latitude $\lambda$, and periodic azimuth
$\theta$. With $r=C\cos^2\lambda/\psi$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{\partial\X}{\partial(\psi,\lambda,\theta)}\right|
 =\frac{C^3\cos^7\lambda}{\psi^4},\qquad
 B\dd\X=
 \frac{C\cos\lambda\sqrt{1+3\sin^2\lambda}}{\psi}
 \dd\psi\dd\lambda\dd\theta.
 \label{eq:dipolefluxmeasure}
\end{equation}
An independent Cartesian automatic-differentiation determinant checks both
identities at two nontrivial coordinates and verifies the inverse flux map.
The finite domain is $0.4\le\psi\le1$, $|\lambda|\le\pi/6$,
$|u|\le4$, $0\le\mu\le12$; $C=1$ and
$f_* =\exp(-E-0.2\mu)$. Angular integration is exact over $2\pi$.

Let conditional means under $f_*\dd\Gamma$ at fixed $\mu$ be denoted by
an overbar, and set
\begin{equation}
 r_E=E-\overline E_\mu,\quad
 r_\psi=\psi-\overline\psi_\mu,\quad
 r_2=\psi^2-\overline{\psi^2}_\mu.
\end{equation}
Solve the positive two-dimensional covariance system
\begin{equation}
 \int f_*\begin{pmatrix}r_E^2&r_Er_\psi\\r_Er_\psi&r_\psi^2\end{pmatrix}
 \dd\Gamma\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
 =\int f_*\begin{pmatrix}r_Er_2\\r_\psi r_2\end{pmatrix}\dd\Gamma,
 \qquad h=r_2-a r_E-b r_\psi.
 \label{eq:fluxcounterexampleprojection}
\end{equation}
The Gram matrix is nonsingular on this positive product domain: an affine
relation between $E$ and $\psi$ would require a vanishing $u^2$ coefficient,
then a constant flux. The residual $h$ is nonzero because $\psi^2$ cannot be
an affine function of $\psi$ on a nonzero-width flux interval after the
$u^2$ coefficient has vanished.
Define $f_\pm=f_*(1\pm\epsilon h)$. The conservative bound
\begin{equation}
 |h|\le(\psi_{\max}^2-\psi_{\min}^2)
      +|a|E_{\max}+|b|(\psi_{\max}-\psi_{\min})=:H
\end{equation}
holds over the entire truncated domain, since conditional means lie in the
same global intervals as their observables. Taking $\epsilon=0.4/H$
guarantees $f_\pm\ge0.6 f_*>0$. Conditional centering preserves every
$G(\mu)$; the covariance projection also preserves total energy and mean
flux. Number follows from the moment marginal. Nevertheless, writing
$N=\int f_*\dd\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1N\int\psi^2(f_+-f_-)\dd\Gamma
 =\frac{2\epsilon}{N}\int f_*h^2\dd\Gamma>0.
 \label{eq:fluxcounterexampleidentity}
\end{equation}
Thus the flux populations differ even after matching the mean canonical
toroidal momentum. This is stronger than a mismatch of mean momentum alone.

For mass-normalized distributions let $\rho_\psi$ be the flux pushforward
density. The conditional Jensen inequality gives the information bound
\begin{equation}
 \frac1N\int f\ln(f/f_*)\dd\Gamma
 \ge\int\rho_\psi\ln(\rho_\psi/\rho_{\psi,*})\dd\psi.
 \label{eq:fluxinformationfloor}
\end{equation}
Conservation of the full flux population fixes its strictly positive
right-hand side. Therefore any closed evolution satisfying
\eqref{eq:fluxpopulation} cannot reach $f_*$ from $f_+$.
Ten independent quadratures refine each integration coordinate and all
coordinates jointly. The $32\times24\times32\times32$ base and
$40\times30\times40\times40$ joint refinement give
$\Delta\langle\psi^2\rangle=2.5868566\times10^{-4}$ and a flux information
floor of $1.5140605\times10^{-5}$ per particle. Discrete number, energy,
each moment-bin population and mean flux match to rounding; the independent
squared-residual identity has about $4\times10^{-12}$ relative discrepancy.
These are matched \emph{discrete} constraints; replacing sums by conditional
integrals gives the continuous construction. The quadrature convergence is
on the stated finite domain, not a tail limit.

The finite-domain distributions are not asserted to satisfy a confined
ideal evolution. In particular, $u=\pm4$ walls are not tangent to the
mirror force. Collision-only natural boundaries suffice for their local
invariant algebra; the combined theorem separately requires closed
flux-compatible ideal boundaries. Specular reversal of $u$ at latitude
endcaps preserves energy, moment and flux, but does not by itself close
finite-$u$ walls. No kinetic trajectory, approach rate or physical dipole
confinement time is inferred from this constructive obstruction.
